# Customer discovery and outreach rules

When the user asks for SWARM outreach, prospect messages, interview questions, follow-ups, market interpretation, or strategy updates from operator responses, read `docs/customer-discovery.md` first and follow it.

Key defaults:

- Do not describe SWARM as "larger fleets of small autonomous aircraft" unless the user explicitly wants that hypothesis tested.
- Do not frame the company around cheap drones replacing larger aircraft.
- Frame the problem around operational objectives, required capabilities, available physical capacity, allocation, composition, retasking, and decision authority.
- With existing UAS operators, explicitly distinguish: required output/data product -> required sensor/payload capability -> compatible platform -> final asset allocation.
- Do not assume capability compatibility and final allocation are the same decision.
- Ask about the operator's real current workflow before pitching the solution.
- Prefer one short question that can be answered asynchronously in one or two lines.
- Avoid em dashes and startup/AI marketing language in outbound messages.
- Never treat one response as a validated wedge or as sufficient reason for a product feature.
- Separate evidence into supports, contradicts, new lead, and unknown.
- Update messaging quickly when a framing is misleading; update architecture only when repeated evidence or a core contradiction justifies it.
- Keep the canonical product invariant intact: SwarmOS decides. Physical agents execute.

The current evidence note in `docs/customer-discovery.md` records two useful signals from a DOI aviation/UAS operator: heterogeneous capability composition is a stronger framing than small-airframe substitution, and one real workflow derives sensor/payload needs from the required data product before selecting compatible UAS. It still does not establish painful allocation, multi-agent composition, delegated mission authority, or a validated wildfire wedge.
